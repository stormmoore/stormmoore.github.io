Game Project: The Bardo Experiment



This game was created by Stormie Moore, Brandon Williams, Alana Howard (Wrin32), and Alyssa Lueking.

This game was created in Spring 2025, during my second semester in freshman year at Boise State University. This game is a 2D interactive comic and platformer game created in Unity 6.The Bardo Experiment, named after the Buddhist term signifying the intermediate state between life and death, combines the quantum physics theory of the emptiness of an atom with the cycles of grief through comic panels spread throughout the game. The main character is trapped in a simulation where scientists are trying to understand the qunatum theory topic, and he is processing his own death that happened before the game starts. This page includes the design process, backgrounds, comic panels, and explanations on my group's thought process during its creation. My position on the team was Sprite and Background Artist, with some help in the coding regarding menu UI and transitions between comic panels and levels.


The Bardo Experiment is a game that I am very proud of. It was the first game that I had spent significant time planning and researching for, to properly explore the topic chosen for my team. The first five weeks of the semester were spent in meetings with everyone, going over quantum theory and the stages of grief in detail. We chose to combine the emptiness of an atom with the stages because of their similarities as 4th dimensional ideas. The "layers" of an atom could be broken down into the different stages of grief, helping the player better understand the concept of the game. It also helped with giving defined gameplay Stages/Levels and clear progression. The Stages/Levels started from the end of the grief cycle, while the player experience follows the generic model to show his growth over the course of the game. Every piece of the game is meant to be symbolic, either representing quantum theory or emotions related to grief. I had the pleasure of coming up with most of the final designs based on the research and ideas my team had come up with together.

This is the collection of comic panels I was in charge of for the game. Each member of the team had to create at least 4 animated panels, with me making 5. The beginning panels showing the main character play before the Denial Stage at the end of the game. The two last panels are about what is happening outside of the simulation, the first being of the scientists watching the experiment. The car panel was the original scrapped idea, of how the main character had passed.



This is the main menu UI background, the parallax background for the Acceptance Stage/Level, and the collectibles for the game. The main menu has easter eggs referencing certain parts of the game you can discover, including a sneak peek into a level's solution. Acceptance was given the color green to represent growth and healing. I was asked to design a parallax background specifically so that felt like the player was running through a place on the edges of civilization. The trees were designed after atom models and electron patterns. The collectibles were designed to be simple and abstract, matching the colors of each Stage/Level. The crystals were exclusive to the Bargaining Stage/Level.

These are the backgrounds for the Bargaining and Denial Stages/Levels, and the sprite sheet for the Denial Stage/Level. Bargaining was given the color purple representing frustration, but also royalty and disconnection. This was the only true platforming level, designed after an abandoned library. Each piece could be placed as an individual asset. Denial was given the color orange, representing anxiety and sometimes joy. This is the final level of the game (my assigned level), where the player character has finally reached acceptance of his death and is ready to move on, but the world is not ready to let him go/leave the simulation. The sprites have a ghostly and goopy design- to show they are not real people, wearing masks to hide their true intentions. The last image shows the unique stained glass windows that serve as portals to the other levels in the game. You must collect the secret item in each level in order to complete the game, changing the portals from the original grey to the themed color ones.

These are some of the storyboards I created for the Denial Stage/Level, including the final boss interaction and the comic panels.
